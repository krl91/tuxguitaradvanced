## 🎸 TuxGuitarAdvanced 0.8.0-beta.6 — hear your guitar through an amp and pedals, and a faster recognition engine

TuxGuitarAdvanced adds **practice modes** on top of [TuxGuitar](https://www.tuxguitar.app/), the free multitrack tablature editor: it listens to your real guitar, checks the **pitch of the notes and the chords** you play against the tablature, and lets you practise note by note, in time (Follow, Arcade) or waiting for the right note.

### 🔖 About this release

A **pre-release**, published before the author has finished checking it. Two pieces of work are in it:

* the **amp and effects** (spec 022), finished and tested, but not yet tried by the author with the Rocksmith cable and headphones;
* the **native recognition engine** (spec 023), whose recognition is finished and measured. Its last measurements (Arcade at the screen's refresh rate) and the author's final check are still to come.

Every feature is in all three packages (macOS, Linux and Windows), with the same behaviour. The macOS package still needs **macOS 15 Sequoia or later**.

> 🙏 **Windows and Linux users: your feedback is needed.** The author has no Windows or Linux computer at hand, so these packages are built by the CI but **have never been run**. This release brings native audio code on each system, both to play your guitar back and to recognise it, and it has never been tried on a real Linux or Windows machine. If you try a package, please open an [issue](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/issues) with your system, its version and your audio interface, and tell:
>
> 1. **The recognition engine** — in the practice settings, the *Recognition engine* should say *Automatic* and use the native engine. If a message says the Java engine is used instead, copy it.
> 2. **The delay of the amp sound** — open *Amp and effects…* in the practice window, turn on *Amp sound* with your guitar plugged in and headphones on, and write down the value of the **Delay** line. Does the delay between the string and the sound bother you?
> 3. **No clicks** — pick the four sounds and move the knobs while playing: each change should be heard at once, without a click.
> 4. **A howl** — with a microphone and speakers (no headphones), turn the sound on and raise the volume: the howl should be cut within half a second, with a message.
> 5. If you can, start the application from a terminal and copy the lines that begin with `TuxGuitar amp sound:` and `TuxGuitar recognition:`.
>
> The full checklist is the sheet [F12](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/blob/master/specs/test-sheets/F12-ampli-effets.md), rows **X1** to **X4**.

### ✨ What is new since 0.8.0-beta.5

**Your guitar through an amp and pedals**

* **An amp, with the score.** While the practice window is open, the new **Amp and effects** window plays your guitar through a virtual amp and its cabinet, mixed with the score. The amp is *Clean*, *Crunch* or *High gain*, with gain, bass, middle, treble and volume. It is **off by default**.
* **Effect pedals:** noise gate, compressor, overdrive and distortion before the amp; chorus, echo and reverb after it. Add, remove, switch and reorder them; every change is heard at once, without a click.
* **Sounds:** four ready-made sounds (*Clean*, *Crunch*, *Lead*, *Clean echo*) and your own, saved, renamed and deleted. A changed ready-made sound shows *(changed)* until *Restore*.
* **A sound per score:** the sound you pick comes back each time you practise that score, before any run.
* **Safe by default:**
  * a warning before the sound starts with the built-in microphone;
  * a howl cut within half a second;
  * a lost device stops the sound while the practice goes on;
  * the chain is made lighter, step by step, if the computer cannot keep up;
  * the output never goes above −1 dBFS.
* **The judging never changes:** the amp sound has its own input. The recognition hears exactly the same thing with the sound on or off.

**A native recognition engine**

* **A second recognition engine, written in Rust**, beside the Java one. It is chosen in the practice settings (*Recognition engine*):
  * *Automatic* (the default) uses the native engine wherever its library loads;
  * *Native (Rust)*;
  * *Java (FastYin)*.
* **Never left without recognition.** When the native engine cannot start, the Java engine takes over, with a message saying why: library missing, input not found, format not supported, stream failed, or an input that does not answer within 1.5 s. A refused microphone permission is reported as such, without a switch.
* **The verdict comes sooner:** on the author's Mac, the native engine gives its verdict about 20 to 30 ms earlier than the Java engine (see below).
* **Chords:** both engines now refuse a chord played a whole tone away from the one written, which they often accepted with distortion or other effects. A few more chords played through effects are recognised.

Texts and tooltips are in English, French and Spanish, and the user guide has a new section on the amp.

### ✅ What was checked, and by whom

* **Tests on the author's Mac:** the full macOS build is green.
  * 1,437 practice tests and 515 audio-engine tests, 0 failures, with each engine.
  * All 79 tests on the real SWT toolkit pass.
* **Both engines on the datasets** (GuitarSet, IDMT-SMT-Guitar, IDMT-SMT-Audio-Effects, the author's chords):
  * the native engine gives exactly the same results as the Java engine;
  * no line is lower than before this release;
  * the chord a whole tone higher is now refused 100 % of the time instead of 66.7 % in IDMT-SMT-Audio-Effects, and chords played through effects are recognised 97 % of the time instead of 95.5 %.
* **The recognition delay on the author's Mac**, from a tone in the speakers to its verdict (median):

  | Input | Java engine | Native engine |
  |---|---|---|
  | Built-in microphone | 139 to 144 ms | about 122 ms |
  | USB microphone | 114 ms | 87 ms |

  The Rocksmith cable has not been measured yet.
* **Ten minutes** of live native recognition without a single dropped block.
* **The amp sound's delay on the author's Mac**, measured with clicks through the native audio code:
  * about **33 ms** with a USB microphone into the built-in speakers (128-frame blocks);
  * **61 ms** with the built-in microphone, whose own hardware adds most of it.

  Ten minutes of the heaviest chain on a real-time audio thread dropped no block; the chain uses about 4 % of each block.
* **Not checked by anyone yet:**
  * the amp sound and the recognition delay with the **Rocksmith cable** and headphones (the author's next check);
  * Arcade at 120 frames a second with the native engine;
  * both features on real **Linux and Windows** machines, whose packages were never run (built only).

### 🚧 What is **not** fixed

* Many **chords are still refused** when they are played: about 6 in 10 of the author's chords through the Rocksmith cable are accepted, and about 4 to 6 in 10 in GuitarSet. Improving them is the next piece of work.
* Some **neighbouring chords are still accepted** in place of the written one (13 of 42 of the author's takes).
* Notes and chords whose **attack is not heard** (a soft note, a low input level) are still refused.
* A chord with **one string missing** can still be accepted.
* The amp sound is offered only while the practice window is open. It is not recorded, and the bass uses the same chain as the guitar.
* **Bluetooth headphones** add their own delay: use wired ones.
* The macOS package is **not notarised**: the first launch needs *Open Anyway* (System Settings → Privacy & Security). The Windows package needs Java 17 or later.

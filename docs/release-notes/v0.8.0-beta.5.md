## 🎸 TuxGuitarAdvanced 0.8.0-beta.5 — sparks on the score, an Advanced tab for both fires, and Reduce motion everywhere

TuxGuitarAdvanced adds **practice modes** on top of [TuxGuitar](https://www.tuxguitar.app/), the free multitrack tablature editor: it listens to your real guitar, checks the **pitch of the notes and the chords** you play against the tablature, and lets you practise note by note, in time (Follow, Arcade) or waiting for the right note.

### 🔖 About this release

A **pre-release** that completes spec 019 over 0.8.0-beta.4. Everything else is the same as 0.8.0-beta.4, including the dark Arcade view in light mode.

Every feature is in all three packages (macOS, Linux and Windows), with the same behaviour. The macOS package still needs **macOS 15 Sequoia or later** and is signed with the same certificate as before.

> 🙏 **Windows and Linux users: your feedback is needed.** The author has no Windows or Linux computer at hand, so these packages are built by the CI but **have never been run**. This release is the first one that reads *Reduce motion* on Windows and Linux, and that has never been tried on a real machine. If you try a package, please open an [issue](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/issues) with your system and its version (for example *Ubuntu 24.04 GNOME*, *Fedora 41 KDE Plasma 6*, *Windows 11 23H2*) and tell what you see:
>
> 1. **Reduce motion** — turn your system's setting on, reopen the practice window, and start a run with the *Score fire* on *Full*: the fire should stand still and throw no spark (only its height follows your playing). Turn the setting back afterwards.
>    * **GNOME**: `gsettings set org.gnome.desktop.interface enable-animations false` (and `true` to undo).
>    * **KDE Plasma**: System Settings → Workspace → animation speed set to *Instant*.
>    * **Windows**: Settings → Accessibility → Visual effects → *Animation effects* off.
>    * Other desktops (Cinnamon, XFCE, …) are not read yet: say so if you use one.
> 2. **The score fire and the Advanced tab** — does the fire grow at the bottom of the score with your right notes and fall at a mistake; does the Advanced tab show its previews, tooltips and *Default values*?
> 3. **Anything else** that works or does not (launch, microphone, tuner, practice modes, Arcade).
>
> The full checklist is the sheet [F11](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/blob/master/specs/test-sheets/F11-feu-partition.md), rows **L1**, **L2**, **W1** and **X1**.

### ✨ What is new since 0.8.0-beta.4

* **Sparks on the score.** With the *Score fire* on (Mode tab, *Discreet* or *Full*), each right note in *Wait* and *Follow the tempo* throws a burst of sparks from the bottom of the fire, bigger with a longer streak, gone within about a second. They never cover the framed note, the next note or the palette.
* **An *Advanced* tab** in the practice window, with two groups: *Arcade fire* and *Score fire*.
  * Every value that shapes each fire can be tuned: opacity, size of the pixels, height at the corners and in the middle, flicker, sway, embers, growth, the sparks and the colours (47 settings for Arcade, 48 for the score).
  * Each group has a **live preview**, a *Sparks* button, a *Growth* slider and **Default values**.
  * Every setting has a tooltip in English, French and Spanish: what it changes, its unit and its default.
  * A value is kept at once and used at the next run of its mode. With the default values, both fires look exactly as before.
  * Whatever the values, the fire never covers what you have to read, and the judging does not change.
* ***Reduce motion* is read on macOS, Linux and Windows**, for both fires and the previews: the fire stands still and throws no spark. On Linux (GNOME, KDE) and Windows the system is asked in the background, so a run never waits for it.

### ✅ What was checked, and by whom

* **Tests on the author's Mac:** the full macOS build is green: 1,401 practice tests and 378 audio-engine tests, 0 failures. All 50 tests on the real SWT toolkit pass, including the Advanced tab. New tests check, for every setting at its minimum and at its maximum, that the fire never covers a protected text or note and that the verdicts do not change.
* **By the author, on macOS:** the score fire and the Advanced tab on the built application ("ça me semble bon").
* **The fire's own painting** at *Full* takes 2.8 to 3.3 ms per picture in the middle of the range, and 4.0 to 4.4 ms for the slowest 5 %, slightly above the 4 ms goal; the author accepted it. On a slower computer the fire simplifies itself, then goes out, before the practice suffers.
* **Not checked by anyone yet:** *Reduce motion* on real Linux and Windows machines (only against a simulated system); the **Windows and Linux packages were never run** (built only).

### 🚧 What is **not** fixed

* Notes and chords whose **attack is not heard** (a soft note, a low input level) are still refused.
* A chord with **one string missing** can still be accepted.
* *Reduce motion* is not read on Linux desktops other than GNOME and KDE.
* The macOS package is **not notarised**: the first launch needs *Open Anyway* (System Settings → Privacy & Security). The Windows package needs Java 17 or later.

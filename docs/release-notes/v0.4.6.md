## TuxGuitarAdvanced 0.4.6

The written techniques (hammer-on, pull-off, slide, vibrato, bend) are explained and followed more completely in the practice mode, the practice says when it cannot follow, and two start-up errors are fixed.

**Packages:** macOS (Apple Silicon) and Windows are attached to this release. **The Linux package is not attached yet**: GitHub refused to start its build because of an Actions budget limit on the project's account. It will be added to this release as soon as it can be built.

### New

* **The written techniques in practice, all of them.** For each of **hammer-on, pull-off, slide, vibrato and bend** the practice listens to the pitch of what you play and follows how it moves; the table below says exactly what it recognises, what it requires and what it does not check yet. In short: the arrival note of a hammer-on, a pull-off or a slide is accepted without asking for a new pick; the vibrato never blocks a note; and **bends (with their release and pre-bend) are now followed along their written path and required**, with a message that tells you what to do next, in English, French and Spanish. **Skip** passes any note you do not want to play.
* **The practice says when it cannot follow.** It tells you when notes are closer than it can follow (about 0.3 s between picked notes), shows the measured delay of the recognition in the preparation window, and warns when the input is too weak for reliable fast recognition.
* **Inputs.** Inputs are accepted, shown and selectable whatever their type (built-in, USB, Bluetooth): nothing is refused because of a name.

### How the practice recognises the written techniques

The practice **listens to the pitch** of what you play, and only to that: it cannot tell the string or the fret from the sound, so those stay an instruction of the tablature. For the techniques written in the score it follows **how the pitch moves**:

| Written technique | What the practice recognises in 0.4.6 | State of the *heard / not heard* verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Hammer-on** | The note you hammer onto is accepted **from its pitch, without a new pick**: a legato note is not asked to be picked again. Measured on synthetic signals at about 0.1 s after the step. | The legato detector exists but has **never been measured on a real recording** (no annotated dataset of hammer-ons or pull-offs was available): the line says *cannot be checked yet*. |
| **Pull-off** | The same, on the way down: the lower note is accepted from its pitch without a new pick. | Same as the hammer-on. |
| **Slide** | The note the slide arrives on is accepted from its pitch, without a new pick. A slide played as steps (as some notation programs render it) can still show its middle note as a wrong note. | Detector measured on 252 annotated recordings: 48 % of the slides, against a target of 85 %: not validated, the line says *cannot be checked yet*. |
| **Vibrato** | **Not required**: the note is accepted from its pitch whether or not you play the vibrato, and it never blocks. | Detector measured at 59 % (target 85 %): not validated, *cannot be checked yet*. |
| **Bend** | **New.** The note starts on its fretted pitch; the practice follows the pitch rising along the written path (a bend value is a quarter of a tone) and **accepts the note when it reaches the target** and holds it (30 cents or 15 % of the bend below, half a semitone above). Until then it says *"now bend it up to Y"*; no pitch on the way is a wrong note, and the pitch the bend reaches is not taken for the next note. | Detector measured at 82 % of the bends (target 85 %): not validated for a verdict. What is enforced is the **pitch path**, which comes from the pitch the practice measures reliably. |
| **Pre-bend** | **New.** The **bent** pitch is expected from the first sound (the string is bent before it is struck). | Same as the bend. |
| **Release** | **New.** After the rise, the pitch must **come down to the written end** before the note is accepted: *"now release the bend down to Y"*. | Same as the bend. |
| **Palm mute, tremolo picking, tapping, harmonics** | Not judged yet (a written harmonic is passed automatically). | Not available. |

For each of them the practice also shows a **technique line** under the pitch result. In this version it says *cannot be checked yet* for every technique, because the detectors that would say *heard* or *not heard* are **not validated enough on real guitar recordings to show a verdict**: do not read that line as a failure. What the practice enforces is what comes from the pitch (the arrival note of a legato or a slide, the path of a bend); it does not judge *how* a technique was made (a finger vibrato inside a bend, for instance, cannot be told from the pitch alone).

### Fixes

* **An error box (`ConcurrentModificationException`) could appear when a Guitar Pro file was opened at start-up.** The tool bar read the song while it was still being loaded; it now waits for the loading to finish.
* **A `NullPointerException` of the built-in synthesizer at every file opening** (a race of the upstream code: the soundbank of the replaced song answered after it was closed). Stale answers are now ignored and the instruments of the new song are loaded.

### Under the hood (not usable yet)

* The groundwork of the tempo-following mode: the player's clock and the capture's clock, the timeline of the score and the timing tolerances (strict, normal, lenient, provisional values). Nothing of it is shown in the application yet.
* A player-only mute of a track in the player (nothing changes unless it is asked).

### Honest limits

* **No technique detector is verified yet**: the technique line says *cannot be checked yet* for hammer-on, pull-off, slide, vibrato and bend, and never says *heard*. What this version adds is that bends are followed and required along their pitch path; the other techniques are accepted from their pitch as described above, not judged for accuracy.
* **Validated on a guitar by the owner:** bends rising and descending and a pre-bend with a release, on test scores. **Not validated on a guitar:** hammer-on, pull-off, slide, vibrato, a bend deliberately not played, and other guitars and microphones. The hammer-on, pull-off and slide figures above come from synthetic signals and from recordings of effects, not from a played score.
* A pre-bend **without** a release has no arrow in the TuxGuitar score, so it looks like a plain note while the practice expects the bent pitch.
* A guitar tuned about 35 cents sharp or more passes plain notes only at the edge of the tolerance: tune with the tuner first.
* The Android build was not run for this release (no Android SDK on the development machine); the packages are macOS (Apple Silicon) and Windows for now, Linux to follow.

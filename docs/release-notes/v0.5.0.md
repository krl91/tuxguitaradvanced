TuxGuitarAdvanced adds **practice modes** on top of [TuxGuitar](https://www.tuxguitar.app/), the free multitrack tablature editor: it listens to your real guitar, checks the **pitch** you play and the **timing**, and tells you right away.

### 🎯 What 0.5.0 is

**0.5.0 is the first release of the 0.5 line without the pre-release label.** It gathers everything of the alpha and beta versions: the **practice modes** (*Wait for the right note*, *Follow the tempo* and **Arcade**, the game with the notes landing at the foot of the neck, its count-in, its result window and the drawings of the bends and techniques), the **official logo**, and the two additions below. The label changes; **what has been checked does not**: see *What was checked, and by whom* below, which says plainly what nobody has looked at yet.

### ✨ What is new since 0.5.0-beta.1

* **A start window, before every start of a mode** (the Start button, the Practice button and the coloured Arcade button of the transport bar):
  * it first asks **"Is your guitar in tune?"**. *No, open the tuner* opens the tuner and the mode does **not** begin; when you close the tuner the question comes back. *Yes* goes on;
  * it then asks you to **strum a chord** and judges the level of the 0.3 seconds that follow. If more than **0.5 % of the samples hit the maximum**, it says the signal **clips**, and tells you what to lower; a signal that is too loud or too weak is named too. A good level starts the mode at once;
  * a bad level **blocks** the start. *Check again* judges a new strum; *Continue anyway* goes on **for this start only** (it is also offered after 10 seconds without a strum);
  * restarting a run, or *play again* from the result window, **does not ask again**. The window never changes the gain of your interface or of the system.
* **Why: chords that were not recognised.** I recorded my own chords through a Rocksmith USB adapter (42 takes). With the input at full volume, so that the signal clipped, only **8 of 21** takes were accepted for the chord that was played; with the volume lowered to 0.40, **18 of 21**. So a good part of the "my chord is not recognised" cases came from a **too-loud input**, not from the playing. The start window now **finds that before you start and tells you how to fix it**. The threshold was calibrated on those takes (19 of 21 flagged at full volume, none at 0.40) and on the 180 microphone recordings of the public GuitarSet dataset (none flagged).
* **Stage flames in Arcade** (*Mode* tab, line **Stage flames**: Off / **Discreet** (the default) / Full): flames burn behind the picture and **grow as you play better**: embers at 50 % of the maximum score or less, full flames at 100 %. They rise in half a second and die down in three. They sit at the foot of the neck, strongest at the corners and weak in the middle, and fade near the string names, the fret numbers and the verdict so that nothing you have to read is covered. They are **never** part of the judging: a test plays the same run with the flames off, discreet and full and the judgements, the score and the result are identical. On macOS they stay still when *Reduce motion* is on in the system settings.

### ✅ What was checked, and by whom

* **Tests:** the full build is green (audio engine 291 tests, more than 900 practice unit tests and 28 native window tests). **Every recognition figure is equal to what it was before** (the same reports over the 180 GuitarSet recordings, the technique datasets and my takes: 26 of 42 accepted, as before): **the recognition code itself was not changed in this beta**. What changed is that a saturated input is now detected and explained.
* **macOS (Apple Silicon):** I tried the start window on the built application. It showed three bugs on the first try (a window over the question, the tuner refusing to open, an empty window after *Yes*); they are fixed and each one has a test now.
* **Not checked by anyone yet:** a full play session with the flames (nobody has played one with them), their cost over a long run (the ten-minute measurement was not made), and everything on **Windows and Linux**, where the advice uses the macOS wording and the *Reduce motion* preference is not read.

### 🚧 What is **not** fixed

* The **D major** chord is refused on my takes even when the signal does not clip: a recognition weakness that this release does not touch.
* A chord with **one string missing** is still accepted in many cases (43 of 78 on synthetic signals). 
* There is **no recorded-audio test** for a really played hammer-on or pull-off, palm mute, tapping or tremolo picking: they are tested on synthetic signals only.

### 🎮 Try it in 30 seconds

1. Open any tablature (a Guitar Pro file works) and put the cursor on the track you want to play.
2. Click the **coloured button** in the transport bar (or *Practise this track*): the start window asks if your guitar is in tune.
3. Answer *Yes*, strum a chord, and see what it says about your level.
4. In the *Mode* tab, choose **Arcade** and try **Discreet** and **Full** flames.

### ⚠️ Honest notes before you start

* **macOS on Apple Silicon only** is looked at by a human for now: the 0.5.0 label does not mean that Windows and Linux were tried. The app is signed ad hoc and **not notarised**: the first time, allow it in *System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway*. It asks for the **microphone** to listen to your guitar.
* The game **judges timing and pitch**. It does **not** tell you whether a hammer-on, a slide or a bend was *heard*: the detectors are not validated yet.
* You cannot hear your own guitar through the app (use your amp or headphones on your interface).

### 🙏 Built on TuxGuitar

All the editing, playback and file formats come from **TuxGuitar** and its many contributors; this fork only adds the practice modes and a steadier tuner. If you only want a superb editor, [go and use TuxGuitar](https://www.tuxguitar.app/) too.

### 🇫🇷 En français

**0.5.0 : les modes d'entraînement, votre guitare vérifiée avant chaque départ, et des flammes sur scène.** C'est la première version de la ligne 0.5 sans l'étiquette « pré-release » : elle reprend tout ce que contenaient les alphas et les bêtas, mais **ce qui a été vérifié n'a pas changé** (voir plus haut ce que personne n'a encore regardé). Avant de lancer un mode, une petite fenêtre demande si la guitare est accordée (sinon, elle ouvre l'accordeur et le mode ne commence pas), puis demande de gratter un accord et juge le niveau : si plus de 0,5 % des échantillons touchent le maximum, elle dit que le signal sature et explique quoi baisser. Un mauvais niveau bloque le départ ; *Continuer quand même* ne vaut que pour ce départ. Relancer une partie ne redemande rien.

Pourquoi : sur mes propres accords (adaptateur Rocksmith, 42 prises), seules **8 prises sur 21** étaient reconnues quand le signal saturait, contre **18 sur 21** avec le volume baissé à 0,40. Une bonne part des accords « non reconnus » venait donc d'une entrée trop forte. **Le code de reconnaissance n'a pas changé** : tous ses chiffres sont identiques à ceux d'avant ; ce qui change, c'est que la saturation est détectée et expliquée. L'accord de **Ré** reste mal reconnu même sans saturation.

Les **flammes** (onglet *Mode* d'Arcade : Aucune / Discrètes par défaut / Pleines) grandissent quand vous jouez mieux et ne gênent pas la lecture ; elles ne changent aucun jugement (testé). L'image ci-dessus est dessinée par le code du jeu, ce n'est pas une capture d'une partie. **Personne n'a encore joué une session complète avec les flammes** : j'ai besoin de vos retours, sur la fenêtre de démarrage comme sur les flammes.

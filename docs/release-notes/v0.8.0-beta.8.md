## 🎸 TuxGuitarAdvanced 0.8.0-beta.8 — chords judged by their notes, windows that fit, and every recognition value tunable

TuxGuitarAdvanced adds **practice modes** on top of [TuxGuitar](https://www.tuxguitar.app/), the free multitrack tablature editor: it listens to your real guitar, checks the **pitch of the notes and the chords** you play against the tablature, and lets you practise note by note, in time (Follow, Arcade) or waiting for the right note.

### 🔖 About this release

A pre-release for feedback. It brings three pieces of work, each tested by the build but **not yet played through by the author** on the built application: the chord check (sheet F15), the practice and amp windows (sheet F14), the expert settings (sheet F16). Every feature is in all three packages (macOS, Linux and Windows), with the same behaviour. The macOS package still needs **macOS 15 Sequoia or later**.

> 🙏 **Windows and Linux users: your feedback is needed.** The author has no Windows or Linux computer at hand: these packages are built by the CI but **have never been run**. If you try one, please open an [issue](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/issues) with your system, its version, your screen height and your audio interface. The sheets [F14](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/blob/master/specs/test-sheets/F14-ergonomie.md), [F15](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/blob/master/specs/test-sheets/F15-accords-tolerants.md) and [F16](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/blob/master/specs/test-sheets/F16-parametres-experts.md) say what to check.

### 📋 Everything new since 0.6.0, the last stable version

From 0.7.0-beta.1 to this release, in short (the details of this release follow below; each earlier pre-release has its own notes):

* **Your guitar through an amp and pedals** (0.8.0-beta.6, reorganised in tabs here):
  * a virtual amp (*Clean*, *Crunch*, *High gain*) and its cabinet, mixed with the score while you practise, off by default;
  * pedals: noise gate, compressor, overdrive, distortion, chorus, echo and reverb;
  * ready-made and personal sounds, with a sound remembered per score;
  * safe by default: the howl guard, the warning with the built-in microphone, the output held under −1 dBFS. The judging never changes, with the sound on or off.
* **A native recognition engine in Rust** (0.8.0-beta.6, checked in 0.8.0-beta.7), beside the Java one, chosen automatically. Its verdict comes about 20 to 30 ms sooner on the author's Mac. If it cannot start, the Java engine takes over with a message.
* **Chords** (this release): judged by their note names over the strum, whatever the voicing, with a *Strict* / *Normal* / *Lenient* check. Since 0.8.0-beta.6, a chord a whole tone away from the written one is refused.
* **Follow the tempo** (0.7.0-beta.1):
  * each refused note says why (no attack heard, wrong note, too early, too late, chord incomplete…), counted in the end summary;
  * an adjustable time to play each note (600 ms by default);
  * its own pitch permissiveness;
  * the rhythm calibration offered before you play;
  * an optional record of a run.
* **Fire and sparks** (0.8.0-beta.1 to 0.8.0-beta.5):
  * Arcade's stage flames became a see-through fire in big pixels, with sparks on every right note;
  * a fire on the score in Wait and Follow grows with your streak of right notes and never covers the note to play;
  * an *Advanced* tab tunes both fires, with a live preview;
  * *Reduce motion* is read on macOS, Linux and Windows.
* **Arcade** (0.8.0-beta.4, 0.8.0-beta.7): it keeps its dark design in light mode, and follows your screen's refresh rate (120 Hz on ProMotion Macs).
* **The practice windows** (0.8.0-beta.1 and this release):
  * the practice palette moves aside when a note comes under it;
  * the windows fit a 768-pixel screen;
  * a greyed start button always says why, and what to do.
* **Expert settings** (this release): every value the recognition uses (192 more) can be tuned in *Advanced* categories, documented, with the former behaviour as default.
* **macOS 15 Sequoia or later** is needed for the macOS package since 0.8.0-beta.3.

### ✨ The chords you play right are accepted, a clearly wrong chord is refused

* **Chords are judged by their note names over the strum.** A chord is accepted when its notes are heard together during the strum, whatever the voicing: another voicing of the same chord (the same note names on other strings, or an octave higher or lower) counts as right. A chord missing at least two of the expected note names is always refused. On the author's reference chords through the Rocksmith cable (Am, C, G, Em, D, A, Bm, Dm), 25 of 25 are accepted and 0 of 408 clearly wrong chords are accepted, with both recognition engines (native and Java). The same check applies in Wait, Follow and Arcade.
* **Chord check, in the Mode tab.** Next to the choice of Wait / Follow / Arcade, **Chord check** offers *Strict*, *Normal* (the default) and *Lenient*; a line under it says what the chosen level accepts and what it risks. *Lenient* accepts more chords; *Strict* refuses more wrong chords and may refuse some right ones. A level you chose is kept across restarts.
* **New chord settings.** In the *Audio recognition* settings, category *Practice — chords*: **Chord rule** (*Note names over the strum*, recommended, or *Every string separately*, the former check), **Chord match**, **Weakest note of a chord** and **Accept a chord on its attack**; under **Chord details**, *Highest note counted* and *Time before judging a chord*. Choosing a level sets three of them, the chord rule, the chord match and the attack; changing one of those three by hand makes the level show *Custom*, and choosing a level again restores its values. Like every recognition setting, they are read at the next start: no restart is needed.

### ✨ The practice and amp windows fit your screen and say what to do

* **The start button is never stuck.** When it is greyed, a line under it (above *Practise this track* and *Close*, on every tab) says why and what to do: no track chosen, the level not checked yet, no signal, a signal that saturates, an input lost, microphone access refused, or a check that is listening. For the input level's causes, **Check again** starts a new check. After *no signal* or *saturates*, the check also starts again by itself as soon as your guitar is heard (never during a run): choosing the input again is no longer needed. This fixes the known issue of 0.8.0-beta.7.
* **The practice window fits a 768-pixel screen.** Each tab scrolls when it is taller than the window, and the start button stays in view. This fixes the known issue of 0.8.0-beta.7.
* **The Amp and effects window, in tabs.** At the top, the check box **Hear my guitar through the amp** (it was *Amp sound*), the sound list with **Save…** and **⋯** (*Rename…*, *Delete*, *Restore*), and the delay. Below, four scrolling tabs: *Pedals before the amp*, *Amp* (with **Speaker simulation**, which was *Cabinet*), *Effects after the amp*, *Output*. *Add pedal* shows the tab of the pedal it adds. A sound's name is asked in a small window, with the same rules as before. The window is at most 600 pixels high at its first opening and never taller than the screen; your settings and sounds are kept.

### ✨ Every recognition value can be tuned without a rebuild (expert settings)

* **192 values that were fixed in the code are now settings**, in 35 new **Advanced** categories of *Configuration › Audio recognition*: the onset detector (normal and fast runs), the pitch and chord spectrum analysers, the struck-note evidence, the input clean-up and the noise gate, the gain suggestion, the Wait and Follow decisions, the playback clock and latency, the timing windows, the Follow and Arcade relaxations, the playing techniques (bend, slide, vibrato, legato), the delay and input calibrations, the level check and the weak-input warning.
* **Nothing changes until you change a value.** Each default is the value the code used before, and the reports of every dataset are identical, with the Java engine and with the native engine.
* **Expert settings.** Their recommended values were measured. Each tooltip says what the value does and which way makes the recognition more lenient or more sensitive. The button *Restore the recommended values of this category* brings them back.
* **When a change applies.** A value the analysis reads (onsets, chord spectrum, input clean-up, noise gate) restarts the listening for a moment. The others apply at the next start.
* **Both engines read the same values.** The native engine receives them in a new tuning array, at the same indices as the Java side. Each package carries its own native library; a native library without the tuning array is refused when it loads and the Java engine is used, with the usual notice.
* **An older configuration file is read as it was.** Every stored value is kept and every new value takes its default, whether it was written by 0.8.0-beta.7 or by a later development build with the chord check levels.
* **The documentation lists everything.** [The engines' documentation](https://github.com/krl91/tuxguitaradvanced/blob/master/docs/architecture/recognition-engines.md) gives the unit, default, range and when it applies for every setting (226 in all). It also lists the constants that stay fixed, with their reason. Tests check this list against the code.

### 🚧 What is **not** fixed

* An **incomplete chord** (one note name fewer than written) may be accepted with *Normal* and with *Strict* alike (about half of them on the public datasets), more often with *Lenient*. **Low chords**, with their lowest note around G2, are harder to recognise: a minor triad on G2 may be refused.
* Notes and chords whose **attack is not heard** (a soft note, a low input level) are still refused.
* **Bluetooth headphones** add their own delay: use wired ones.
* The macOS package is **not notarised**: the first launch needs *Open Anyway* (System Settings → Privacy & Security). The Windows package needs Java 17 or later.
